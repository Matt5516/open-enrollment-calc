Embedded vs non-embedded deductible

With an embedded deductible each person has an individual deductible inside the family deductible, so one person with large bills can reach coverage alone. With a non-embedded (aggregate) deductible the whole family deductible must be met before the plan pays coinsurance for anyone. Neither is always cheaper.

Example family plan: $7,000 family deductible, 20% coinsurance, $12,000 family out-of-pocket maximum. Embedded version: individual deductible $3,500 (the 2027 HSA minimum for family plans), individual out-of-pocket maximum $8,700.

Step by step: one member with $20,000

  • Embedded: they pay the $3,500 individual deductible, then 20% of the remaining $16,500: $3,500 + $3,300 = $6,800.
  • Non-embedded: the family deductible is $7,000, so they pay $7,000 plus 20% of $13,000 = $9,600.

Step by step: four members at $2,000 each

  • Embedded: nobody passes the $3,500 individual deductible, so each pays $2,000: $8,000.
  • Non-embedded: the family pays the first $7,000 of the $8,000, then 20% of $1,000: $7,200.

Rules to know

  • Under the ACA, no one on a family plan can be charged more than the self-only out-of-pocket limit ($12,000 in 2027), which in effect makes the individual part embedded for the out-of-pocket maximum.
  • For an HSA-eligible family plan with an embedded deductible, the individual deductible must also meet the family minimum ($3,500 in 2027). See HDHP minimum deductible.
  • Your Summary of Benefits and Coverage says which kind your plan uses; the labels are often “individual / family”.

FAQ

What is an embedded deductible?

A deductible where each person on a family plan has their own individual deductible within the family deductible.

Is an embedded deductible better?

When one person has big bills it can be: in the example the family pays $6,800 instead of $9,600. When costs are spread across members the non-embedded plan can be cheaper ($7,200 vs $8,000).

Do HSA family plans allow embedded deductibles?

Yes, but the individual deductible must be at least the family minimum ($3,500 for 2027).

How do I know which my plan has?

Check the Summary of Benefits and Coverage or ask your plan; look for “family deductible” and whether an individual limit applies inside it.
