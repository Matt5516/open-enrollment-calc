HSA vs PPO when you are having a baby

A birth almost always hits the deductible, so what usually matters is the out-of-pocket maximum and premiums.

In this made-up example (A couple expecting a baby, enrolling in family coverage for the plan year of the birth), the lower-cost plan is: Uncomplicated delivery year ($12,000 of care): HSA plan by about $290; Delivery with complications ($25,000 of care): PPO by about $410; NICU stay or high-risk pregnancy ($45,000 of care): PPO by about $410. These are illustrations, not recommendations.

What matters in this situation

  • A planned birth makes this a near-certain high-cost year, so compare the worst-case totals ($11,910 vs $11,500), not just the low-use case.
  • The spending levels above are illustrative round numbers for total allowed charges (prenatal care, delivery, newborn care), not national averages; real bills vary widely by hospital and plan.
  • Adding a newborn to your plan is usually a special enrollment event with a short window (often 30 days for job plans, 60 for the marketplace), so check the deadline with HR.
  • HSA contributions are limited month by month: moving from self-only to family coverage when the baby arrives changes your limit; see the last-month rule.
  • Maternity and newborn care are essential health benefits under the ACA, and on in-network care the out-of-pocket maximum caps your share once you reach it. Out-of-network bills may not count toward it.

If you expect the pregnancy to cross two plan years, each year has its own deductible and maximum, so timing of services can change the totals.

FAQ

Is an HSA or a PPO better when having a baby?

In these example numbers: uncomplicated delivery year ($12,000 of care) - hSA plan by about $290; delivery with complications ($25,000 of care) - pPO by about $410; nicu stay or high-risk pregnancy ($45,000 of care) - pPO by about $410. Your own plan numbers decide, so use the calculator with them.

What assumptions does this example use?

Family coverage; HSA plan $400/month with a $5,000 deductible and $9,000 out-of-pocket maximum; PPO $500/month with a $1,500 deductible and $5,500 maximum; 20% coinsurance on both.

Where do these numbers come from?

They are example plans run through the same calculation as the HSA vs PPO calculator. They are not real plans or average costs.

What is the worst-case yearly cost of each plan here?

HSA plan $11,910 and PPO $11,500, after employer money and tax savings.
