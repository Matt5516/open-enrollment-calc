HSA limit in the year you enroll in Medicare (2027)

You can contribute to an HSA only for months before Medicare starts. In 2027, if Medicare begins July 1 and you had self-only HDHP coverage since January, the limit is generally 6 ÷ 12 × $4,500 = $2,250 (plus $500 prorated catch-up if you are 55+). The last-month rule does not help in a year you enroll in Medicare.

Months eligible = months before the month Medicare coverage starts. A month counts if you are eligible on its first day.

Details that surprise people

  • Any Medicare enrollment counts, including premium-free Part A. Enrolling in Part A or B ends eligibility to contribute from the first month of coverage.
  • Part A can be retroactive. If you apply for Medicare or Social Security after 65, Part A coverage can start up to six months back, so contributions made in that window can become excess contributions. Stop contributing about six months before you plan to apply.
  • You can still spend your existing HSA balance tax-free on qualified medical costs, including Medicare premiums (not Medigap).
  • Staying on an employer HDHP past 65 and delaying Medicare is how some people keep contributing; that is a decision for an advisor or the SSA.

Worked example

A 65-year-old has self-only HDHP coverage and Medicare Part B starts August 1, 2027. Months eligible: 7. Limit: $4,500 × 7 ÷ 12 = $2,625, plus catch-up $1,000 × 7 ÷ 12 = $583, for a total of $3,208. If their employer deposits $1,200, they have $2,008 of room.

Sources: IRS Publication 969; Medicare.gov. Values as of October 18, 2026.

FAQ

Can I contribute to an HSA after I enroll in Medicare?

No. Contributions are not allowed for any month you are enrolled in Medicare, but you can keep using the existing balance for qualified expenses.

How is the 2027 HSA limit figured in the year I start Medicare?

Generally 1/12 of the annual limit for each month before Medicare starts, such as $2,250 for six months of self-only coverage.

Does Medicare Part A start retroactively?

It can start up to six months back if you apply after age 65, so contributions in that period may become excess contributions.

Does the last-month rule apply when I enroll in Medicare?

No. It requires you to be eligible on December 1, which you are not once Medicare has started.
